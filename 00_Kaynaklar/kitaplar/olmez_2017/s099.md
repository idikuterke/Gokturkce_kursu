## Summary of page 101 (*Köktürkçe Dersleri 101*)

### Section: Bilge Kağan Inscription (*Bilge Kağan Yazıtı*)

**Historical background:** After his uncle Kapgan Kağan was killed during the war with the Tokuz Oğuz, Bilge Kağan was elevated to the khaganate with the support of Tunyukuk. According to Chinese sources, before taking this title he bore the name *Mojilian* (given with Chinese characters in the text). He was the son of Ēltēriš, founder of the khaganate, and also Tunyukuk's son-in-law. He ruled from 716–734, and after his death an inscription was erected for him as well. The inscription stands about 1 km from his brother's (Kül Tegin's) monument. The text notes that its erection date is recorded by his son Tengri Kağan.

**Quote of the runic passage:** The book gives a transliteration (in italics) of the runic passage stating that the kagan died in the dog year, tenth month, twenty-sixth day, and that the funeral was held in the pig year, fifth month, twenty-seventh day. This is followed by a Turkish translation and the reference **(BK G 10)**.

**Dates:** Accordingly, Bilge Kağan died on **25 November 734**, and the funeral ceremony was held on **22 June 735** ("in the pig year, fifth month, twenty-seventh day").

**On the 12-year animal calendar:** The author notes that this calendar system reached as far as Anatolia but has been completely forgotten there, while the year names are still known and used by Turkic peoples of Central Asia and Siberia, from Turkmenistan to Tuva.

### Subheading: General Appearance of the Inscription (*Yazıtın Genel Görünümü*)

- Like the Kül Tegin inscription, one face is in Chinese and the other three faces are in Turkic.
- The tortoise pedestal is heavily damaged.
- Compared with the Kül Tegin inscription, the stone quality is poorer, so many lines and letters have become very difficult to read.
- Subjected to rain, snow, and frost damage until the 2000s, it is now preserved together with the Kül Tegin inscription in the museum built by TİKA.
- Dimensions: height **369 cm**, width **122–126 cm**. The author refers readers to Alyılmaz 2005, p. 103 and following, for details.

### Subheading: Location (*Yeri*)

- Same site as the Kül Tegin inscription; the distance between the two erected monuments is roughly 1 km.
- GPS data given: 1091378, 8 T 0337319, UTM 5269389 (Alyılmaz, p. 103).
