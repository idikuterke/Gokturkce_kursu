## Suffixes Deriving Nouns from Nouns (İsimen İsim Türeten Ekler)

| Suffix | Description |
|---|---|
| **{+Ak}:** | This is seen in the following example: *çorak* "çorak, salty and dry soil" (BK GD) < \**İran. şör* "salt". |
| **{+An}:** | The words *eren* (er+en) and *oglan* (ogıl+an) must have derived from this suffix. It can probably also be seen in this one example: *yegren* "chestnut color (of a horse's coat)" (KÇ D 3, 9) < *yegir* "gazelle" (KÇ D 6); cf. Hak. *çigren* "chestnut color", Tat. *ciren* id., Yak. *sier* id. < \**yegir*. The modern word *ceren* (< Middle Mo. *ce'eren* < ET *yegren*) must have developed from this. |
| **{+(I)ç}:** | This suffix, which forms diminutive and endearing words, is encountered mostly in kinship terms such as mother, father, etc.: *ataç+ım* "my dear father" (O 12, supplementary inscription 3; Uybat III 11) < *ata* "father"; *içiç+im* "my dear elder brother" (Uyuk-Turan 6, Uybat III 6, etc.) < *ėci, içi* "elder brother"; *begiç+im* "my dear lord" (Abakan 7); *kaŋıç+ım* "my dear father" (Abakan 14). |
| **{+çı}:** | It derives nouns denoting a member of a profession or one who constantly performs a task: *bed(i)zçi* "painter, sculptor" (KT G 11, etc.) < *bediz* "picture, statue"; *evçi* "spouse, wife" (Bay Bulun I 2, etc.) < *ev* "house"; *sıgıtçı* "mourner, wailer" (KT K 11, etc.) < *sıgıt* "mourning, lamentation"; *tamgaçı* "keeper of the seal" (KT K 13) < \**tamga* "stamp, seal"; *yagıçı* |
